Not every effect is about resolution. Plenty of games look better with a little more color, a little more bite, or a completely different mood, and that's what this family of effects is for. They work on the finished image, so they're available for every game, and most of them are cheap enough to leave on all the time.

## The built-in effects

Five effects come with MacLauncher itself and work offline:

- <span id="colour"></span>**Colour**: a saturation control, set a little above neutral (1.2, where 1 leaves color unchanged) by default.
- <span id="sharpen"></span>**Sharpen** and **CAS Sharpen**: classic sharpening, and AMD's Contrast Adaptive Sharpening, which sharpens soft areas more than already-crisp ones. CAS is a nice finishing touch after an upscaler.
- <span id="grayscale"></span>**Grayscale**: exactly what it sounds like.
- **CRT**: covered in [CRT and retro](/maclauncher/graphics/crt-and-retro/).

<span id="combine-effects"></span>**Combine effects…** runs two to four of these as a single row.

- <span id="color-and-tone"></span>**Color and tone:** [SweetFX](https://github.com/CeeJayDK/SweetFX) Tonemap, Vibrance and Curves, color balance, **Histogram exposure** (automatic exposure from the image's own histogram) and [Halide](https://github.com/halide/Halide)'s **local Laplacian** filter for detail and contrast.
- <span id="light-and-lens"></span>**Light and lens:** soft bloom, and AMD [FidelityFX](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK) **Lens** (grain, chromatic aberration, vignette) and **Blur**.
- <span id="clean-up"></span>**Clean-up:** threshold debanding for smooth gradients, and [SMAA](https://github.com/iryoku/smaa) and [CMAA2](https://github.com/GameTechDev/CMAA2) anti-aliasing for jagged edges in games that don't smooth them themselves.
- <span id="artistic"></span>**Artistic:** [anisotropic Kuwahara](https://github.com/jkyprian/gpuakf), the painterly filter.
- <span id="accessibility"></span>**Accessibility:** **Daltonize** simulates color-vision deficiencies and shifts the lost color differences into ones you can see, with adjustable strength.

Shader collections add a lot more: [qUINT](https://github.com/martymcmodding/qUINT) for bloom, debanding and more, [prod80](https://github.com/prod80/prod80-ReShade-Repository) for film-style color grading and LUTs, [CShade](https://github.com/papadanku/CShade) for exposure and image-based motion effects, and [FXShaders](https://github.com/luluco250/FXShaders) for NeoBloom and ArcaneBloom. See [Shader collections and imports](/maclauncher/graphics/shaders/).

## LUTs

A LUT (lookup table) is a complete color grade in a file, the same kind that photo and video editors use. Drop a `.cube` file onto MacLauncher and it joins the shader library as an effect, with controls for strength, trilinear or tetrahedral interpolation, and whether it expects encoded or linear color. Full-size 65-point 3D tables are supported.

## Depth without a depth buffer

Fog that thickens with distance needs to know how far away things are, and most games don't share that. **Estimated depth fog** gets around it: [Depth Anything V2](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2), a small neural network, estimates depth from the finished picture, and the fog follows that estimate. It works with any game on Apple Silicon once the model is installed.

## Effects that use the game's depth

Some lighting effects need the real thing: the game's depth buffer, and sometimes its camera, surface normals and materials. For games whose runtime provides them, MacLauncher includes:

- [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO) and AMD [CACAO](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK) ambient occlusion, which darkens corners and contact points;
- FidelityFX **depth of field**;
- depth fog and depth outlines adapted from FXShaders;
- [Godot](https://github.com/godotengine/godot)'s screen-space reflections;
- through collections, [SSRT3](https://github.com/cdrinmatane/SSRT3) indirect lighting and the [NiceGuy shaders](https://github.com/mj-ehsan/NiceGuy-Shaders)' lighting, reflections and fog.

When a game doesn't provide those inputs, these effects stay listed with a note explaining what they're waiting for.

## Final output

The chain's **Output** section handles the last step to the screen. **Tone mapping** decides how bright highlights are squeezed into the display's range: Automatic, None, Reinhard or PBR Neutral. HDR and wide-gamut images keep their range through the chain, and effects that only understand standard color get a local conversion.

The [shader library section](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/GRAPHICS.md#shader-library) of the graphics guide lists every included effect, and the [provider reference](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/graphics/PROVIDERS.md#shader-formats-and-image-effects) describes each native effect's algorithm.
