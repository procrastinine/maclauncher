For a long time, pixel art was made for television tubes. The artists knew their pixels would be softened by the electron beam, separated by dark scanlines, glowing through a phosphor mask, and they drew with that in mind. (The [CRT Pixels](https://primagames.com/featured/interview-crt-pixels-jordan-starkweather-tells-us-why-retro-games-need-retro-screens) project made this famous with side-by-side close-ups of real tubes.) On a modern display every pixel is a hard-edged square, which is crisp, but not quite what the art was meant to look like. CRT shaders put the tube back.

## The built-in CRT effect

**CRT** is part of MacLauncher and works offline with every game. Its parameters cover the parts of the look you're most likely to want to tune:

- **Simulated content height**: how many scanlines to draw. Set it to the game's original height (240 for many classic games) and the scanlines keep that spacing however you resize the window, or set 0 to follow the image size.
- **Scanline beam width** and **Scanline darkness**: how thick the lit lines are and how dark the gaps between them get.
- **Pixel softness** and **Phosphor glow**: the gentle blur and bloom of a real tube.
- **Screen curvature**, **Rounded corners**, **Edge shading** and **Screen edge fade**: the shape of the glass.

- <span id="crt-royale"></span>**[CRT Royale](https://github.com/akgunter/crt-royale-reshade)**, Alex Gunter's [ReShade](https://reshade.me/) port of TroggleMonkey's famous shader.
- <span id="retroarch-crt-shaders"></span>**CRT Lottes**, **CRT Easymode**, **GTU** and **zfast CRT**, from [RSRetroArch](https://github.com/Matsilagi/RSRetroArch)'s ReShade ports of the [RetroArch](https://www.retroarch.com/) originals.

Several of these were written for emulators, where the shader knows the console's real resolution. They have **Screen** or **Frame** width and height parameters; set those to the game's original resolution so the scanlines line up with its pixels. The Lottes, Easymode, GTU and zfast samples above use Sonic the Hedgehog 2's 320×240. CRT Royale is shown at its default settings.

The [libretro Slang collection](https://github.com/libretro/slang-shaders), available from **Runtimes → Graphics → Shaders → Collections**, adds CRT halation along with pixel-art scalers, dedithering and restoration filters. You can also import any `.slangp` preset you already use in RetroArch, CRT presets included. See [Shader collections and imports](/maclauncher/graphics/shaders/).

## Where a CRT goes in the chain

A CRT effect needs room to draw: several screen pixels for every game pixel. So it belongs *after* the image has been enlarged to the window, usually with a **Nearest** upscale in front of it:

1. **Upscale**, Nearest, to the window.
2. **CRT**, with the game's original height.

Color effects can go before or after it, depending on whether you want to grade the game or the "screen". [Building a chain](/maclauncher/graphics/composing-effects/) has more on ordering.

## Motion clarity

CRTs had one more trick: they drew each frame as a quick flash of light, which made motion look sharp in a way modern displays don't. The main **Motion clarity** control brings some of that back. It offers **Black-frame insertion**, which shows dark frames between real ones, and **CRT beam scheduling**, which spreads each frame over several subframes to imitate a scanning beam. Subframe count, duty and brightness are adjustable, and **Auto** turns it on when a shader in the chain asks for it. Your display's refresh rate decides how much room there is to work with, so high-refresh displays get the most out of it.

The CRT controls are described in the [settings reference](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/runtimes/GRAPHICS.md), and motion clarity in the [generation and output section](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/GRAPHICS.md#generation-and-output) of the graphics guide.
