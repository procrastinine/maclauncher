CRT shaders give pixel-art games on a Mac the look of the cathode-ray tube (CRT) televisions and monitors they were drawn for. For decades, artists making pixel art knew that the electron beam would soften their pixels, that dark scanlines would separate the rows, and that the picture would glow through a phosphor mask, and they drew with that in mind. The [CRT Pixels](https://primagames.com/featured/interview-crt-pixels-jordan-starkweather-tells-us-why-retro-games-need-retro-screens) project made this widely known with side-by-side close-ups of real tubes. On a modern display, by contrast, every pixel is a hard-edged square, which is crisp but differs from what the artists saw.

## The built-in CRT effect

MacLauncher's own **CRT** effect works with every game, and its parameters control the main parts of the look.

**Simulated content height** sets how many scanlines to draw; its default of 240 matches many classic games. Set it to the game's original height and the scanlines keep that spacing however you resize the window, or set it to 0 to follow the image size.

The scanlines themselves are shaped by **Scanline beam width** and **Scanline darkness**, which set how thick the lit lines are and how dark the gaps between them get. **Pixel softness** and **Phosphor glow** add the gentle blur and bloom of a real tube, while **Screen curvature**, **Rounded corners**, **Edge shading** and **Screen edge fade** shape the glass. Finally, **CRT strength** blends the whole effect with the original image.

- <span id="crt-royale"></span>**[CRT Royale](https://github.com/akgunter/crt-royale-reshade)** is Alex Gunter's [ReShade](https://reshade.me/) port of TroggleMonkey's widely used shader.
- <span id="retroarch-crt-shaders"></span>**CRT Lottes**, **CRT Easymode**, **GTU** and **zfast CRT** come from [RSRetroArch](https://github.com/Matsilagi/RSRetroArch)'s ReShade ports of the [RetroArch](https://www.retroarch.com/) originals.

Several of these were written for emulators, where the shader knows the console's real resolution. To get the same result here, set their **Screen** or **Frame** width and height parameters to the game's original resolution, so the scanlines line up with its pixels. The Lottes, Easymode, GTU and zfast samples above use Sonic the Hedgehog 2's 320×240, while CRT Royale is shown at its default settings.

The [libretro Slang collection](https://github.com/libretro/slang-shaders), available from **Runtimes → Graphics → Shaders → Collections**, adds CRT halation along with pixel-art scalers, dedithering and restoration filters. You can also import any `.slangp` preset you already use in RetroArch, including CRT presets. [Shader collections and imports](/maclauncher/graphics/shaders/) describes both.

## Where a CRT goes in the chain

A CRT effect needs several screen pixels for every game pixel to draw its scanlines and mask, so it belongs after the image has been enlarged to the window, usually by a **Nearest** upscale:

1. **Upscale**, Nearest, to the window.
2. **CRT**, with the game's original height.

Color effects can go before or after it, depending on whether you want to grade the game or the simulated screen. [Building a chain](/maclauncher/graphics/composing-effects/) has more on ordering.

## Motion clarity

A CRT also drew each frame as a brief flash of light, whereas modern displays keep each frame lit until the next one replaces it, which makes moving objects look less sharp. To recreate some of the CRT's sharpness, the chain's **Output** section has a **Motion clarity** control. Its **Black frame insertion** shows dark frames between real ones, while **CRT beam** scheduling spreads each frame over several subframes to imitate a scanning beam. **Subframes**, **Visible duty** and **Brightness** adjust the scheduling, and **Auto** turns it on when a shader in the chain asks for it. The display's refresh rate limits how much room there is for subframes, so high-refresh displays benefit most.

MacLauncher's graphics guide lists the CRT effect's parameters under [included effects](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/graphics/README.md#included-effects) and describes [motion clarity](https://github.com/procrastinine/maclauncher/blob/master/docs/graphics/README.md#motion-clarity) in full.
